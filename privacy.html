Privacy Policy

Effective October 16, 2026 · eHut LLC, Quitman, Mississippi

SugarCheck is made by eHut LLC. It is free, has no ads, and keeps your information on your phone. This page says exactly what the app touches and where it goes. If anything here changes, the date above changes and the app's Settings screen links to the new version.

The short version

  • There is no account. We don't know who you are.
  • Your first name, your diabetes type, your reminders and your saved foods stay on your phone. We never see them.
  • Nothing about you is sold, shared, or used for advertising.
  • Three things leave your phone, and only when you ask: a barcode number, the words you type to find a food, and a request for the business directory. None of them carries your name or anything about you.
  • You can erase everything with one tap in Settings → Clear my data.

What the app keeps on your phone

First name and diabetes type. You give these when you set up the app. The name appears in your first reminder of the day. Both live in a small database on your phone and nowhere else.

Reminders. The times and words of your reminders, and whether you tapped Done today. Stored on your phone, scheduled through Android's own notification system.

Saved foods. Any food label you choose to save, with its carbohydrate figures. Stored on your phone.

A lookup cache. Copies of the last 200 food-label results and the business directory, so the app works without a signal. Stored on your phone.

None of this is backed up anywhere by us. If you lose the phone, it is gone. If you uninstall the app, Android deletes all of it.

What leaves your phone, and when

A barcode number — only when you scan one. It goes to Open Food Facts (a non-profit, open food database) so the app can look up that product's label. The request carries the barcode and the app's name, nothing about you. Open Food Facts' own policy: world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy

The words you type to find a food — only when you use "Build a plate" and the app doesn't already know the food. The same database searches for them. The words, nothing else.

A request for the business directory — once a day when the app opens, to download the list of local pharmacies and clinics. The file is hosted on GitHub Pages; like any web request, the hosting provider sees your phone's internet address for the duration of the download. We do not receive or keep it.

That is the complete list.

Things the app uses but never stores or sends

Camera. Used to read a barcode or take a picture of a Nutrition Facts panel. The picture is read on the phone itself and discarded. No photo is saved and no photo leaves the phone.

Location. Asked for only when you tap "Near me" on the Directory screen, used to sort that list by distance, and forgotten when you leave the screen. Never saved, never sent. Decline it and the app sorts by county instead.

Steps (Health Connect). If you allow it, the app reads today's step total from Android's Health Connect each time you open it, shows it on the Home screen, and may add it to the rest of that day's reminders (for example, "Check before dinner? · 4,120 steps as of 2:15 pm"). The app only reads; it never writes to Health Connect, never stores the count, and never sends it anywhere. Turn it off any time in Health Connect's own settings; the Settings screen has a button that takes you there. Health Connect data is used only to show you your own step count and for nothing else. It is never used for advertising, never shared with anyone, and never sold.

Notifications. Reminders are local notifications created by the app on your phone. No notification service, no server.

Glucose and medical information

SugarCheck never asks for, stores, or sends a blood sugar reading, an A1c, a medication name, or a dose. It is an awareness tool, not a medical device, and it is not a substitute for advice from your doctor, nurse practitioner, or dietitian.

Sponsors and the directory

Local businesses pay to be listed in the directory and shown in the sponsor strip. They receive the number of installs reported by the Google Play Store for the app as a whole. They never receive anything about any individual — not who opened the app, who saw their name, who tapped it, or where anyone is. The app does not count or report any of that.

Children

SugarCheck is for adults managing their own diabetes. It is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect anything from them — which, since the app collects nothing from anyone, is also just how it works.

Your choices

  • See everything the app has: it is all on the Settings and Reminders screens.
  • Erase everything: Settings → Clear my data. This deletes your name, type, reminders, saved foods and the cache from the phone. There is nothing anywhere else to erase.
  • Camera, location, notifications, steps: each is a standard Android permission you can revoke in your phone's Settings or in Health Connect. The app keeps working; the feature that needed it goes quiet.

Changes

If a future version sends anything new off the phone, this page will say so before that version is released, with the date updated at the top.
