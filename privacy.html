SugarCheck by eHUT™

Privacy policy

Effective November 4, 2026 · eHUT LLC · Quitman, Mississippi

SugarCheck helps adults understand food labels, organize reminders and find community resources. This policy explains how the Android app handles information, which features contact outside services, and the choices you have.

At a glance

  • No required SugarCheck account. Your profile, saved foods, reminders and optional GLP-1 records are kept in the app's storage on your phone.
  • Some features use outside services. Food lookups, community downloads and Google's scanning diagnostics send information off the phone. These requests can include technical information such as an internet address.
  • You control sharing. When you share a log, Android lets you choose the receiving person or app.
  • Sponsors support the app. Sponsorship does not give a business access to your personal health records.
  • You can clear app data. Settings → Clear my data removes app records and requests cleanup of temporary label photos. Copies already shared outside SugarCheck are handled by their recipients.

Information on your phone

The app stores information you enter or save, including your first name, diabetes category, reminder settings and completion records, saved food and meal information, and feature preferences. It also caches food lookup results and community content so some information remains available offline.

If you enable the GLP-1 companion, it stores the medicine you select, your schedule, shot dates and sites, the strength you enter, symptom selections, notes, weight entries and insurance-preparation checklist selections. Optional height and other feature preferences may also be stored. Free-text notes contain whatever you choose to write.

These records use Android app-private storage. SugarCheck does not provide an eHUT account or cloud synchronization service for these records. Android application backup is disabled in the app configuration. App-private storage should not be understood as a promise of database-level encryption or protection against someone who can access your unlocked or compromised phone.

Camera and label scanning

The camera reads barcodes and captures Nutrition Facts panels. Google's ML Kit barcode and text-recognition components process label images on the device. SugarCheck does not upload your label photos or recognized label text for this scanning feature.

Temporary photos. Taking a label photo creates a temporary file. The app attempts to delete it after processing, including unsuccessful scans. Cleanup can fail, or a crash can interrupt it. The app also attempts camera-cache cleanup when it starts and when you use Clear my data. A temporary file can remain until cleanup succeeds. Clearing app storage or uninstalling the app removes its private storage through Android.

Google's diagnostics. ML Kit collects device and application information, installation identifiers, performance measurements, configuration and feature-event information, and error codes for diagnostics and usage analytics. These identifiers can distinguish an installation even without your name. Google describes transmission over HTTPS and its handling in the ML Kit data disclosure and Google Privacy Policy. This SDK activity is separate from the label image processing on your phone.

Food lookups and community downloads

Open Food Facts. Barcode lookups send the barcode to Open Food Facts. Food searches can send your typed search words when an outside lookup is used. The service receives the request and ordinary network information, such as an internet address and app identification. Search text can reveal what you type. The app does not attach your SugarCheck profile or GLP-1 log to these requests. See the Open Food Facts privacy policy.

GitHub Pages. SugarCheck downloads the directory, announcements, resource links and related configuration from eHUT's GitHub Pages site. It can also download approved sponsor logos and announcement images. Downloads happen when content is refreshed, including automatic refreshes and actions such as pull-to-refresh. Previously downloaded content can be cached on your phone.

The app restricts downloaded community image URLs to eHUT's GitHub Pages host. Hosting providers still receive internet addresses and request information and handle it under their own practices. See the GitHub Privacy Statement. Clearing SugarCheck's local data does not erase records retained by an outside service.

Location. Near me asks for location permission to sort care listings by distance on your phone. That sorting does not send your coordinates to eHUT or include them in food lookups. You can decline location access and use the other directory filters. Opening directions in a map app is a separate action governed by that app's practices.

Health Connect steps and local reminders

If you grant Health Connect step-reading permission, SugarCheck reads today's step total to display it on Home and may include a step-count snapshot in that day's scheduled reminders. Home can refresh the count while it is open. SugarCheck reads steps and does not write step records to Health Connect.

The app does not send Health Connect step information to eHUT or sponsors or use it for advertising. Although SugarCheck does not keep a separate step-history log, a snapshot may remain in a scheduled or delivered notification. Revoking access prevents future permitted reads but does not automatically retract notification copies already created.

Reminders are scheduled locally through Android. Your name, reminder message, or a step snapshot may be visible in notifications, including on the lock screen according to your device settings. You can change notification permissions, quiet hours and lock-screen settings.

Sharing, support and external links

Sharing a log. Share this log with my clinic prepares information from the GLP-1 companion and opens Android's share menu. The export can contain your first name, selected medicine and entered strength, shot history, symptoms, notes, weight, height, calculated BMI and insurance-preparation information. Check the content and select the intended recipient. The receiving app or person may retain or forward a copy under their own practices.

Support and community submissions. Report a problem opens a draft message that can include the app version, device model and Android version. You choose what to add and whether to send it. If you email eHUT, submit a resource or event, or request a business listing, eHUT receives your contact details and whatever information you send. eHUT uses that correspondence to respond, investigate, review or maintain the submission. Please avoid unnecessary patient information in messages. Contact us to request deletion of correspondence. Some records may need to be retained for an unresolved request or applicable recordkeeping obligations.

Approved business details, event information and resource descriptions may be published for other users to see. Submit only information you have permission to make public.

External services. Website, directions, call, registration, social-media and payment links open other apps or services. Those services handle the information you provide under their own policies. Sharing SugarCheck's public link does not attach your personal SugarCheck records. An external checkout handles any payment details you enter there.

Sponsors and paid promotion

SugarCheck includes local supporter recognition and can display paid directory or community placements. Payment does not give a sponsor access to your profile, medication records, reminders, saved foods, Health Connect steps or precise location.

eHUT does not sell your personal app records or use them to select sponsor placements. The app's sponsor display is based on the community directory and placement configuration. Sponsor reporting can include app-wide installation figures available through Google Play. Such figures do not identify who viewed or tapped an individual placement.

If a sponsor promotion is also published on an external social platform, that platform may provide its own aggregate reach or interaction figures. Those figures concern that platform's posts and are separate from SugarCheck health records. The platform's privacy policy applies when you use it.

Your choices, retention and deletion

Local records. Records remain in app storage until you change or delete them, clear app data, or remove the app. Cached information can also be replaced as content is refreshed.

Clear my data. In Settings, this control cancels pending SugarCheck reminders and deletes the local profile, saved foods, reminders, GLP-1 records, preferences and app caches. It also attempts to remove remaining temporary camera files. If cleanup fails or you still see information, use Android's app-storage controls or uninstall SugarCheck.

This action does not remove logs already shared with another person or app, source step records in Health Connect, emails already sent, public submissions, or outside-service records. Manage those copies with the relevant recipient, service or eHUT contact address. Previously delivered notifications can be dismissed through Android.

Permissions. You can revoke camera, location and notification permissions in Android settings and step-reading permission in Health Connect. Features that need the permission will be limited. Other app features remain available.

Security, adult use and policy changes

SugarCheck relies on Android's app-storage and permission controls. Its food and community network endpoints use HTTPS, and Google's ML Kit documentation describes HTTPS transmission for SDK diagnostics. No device or transmission method can guarantee complete security. Keep your phone protected with a screen lock and current security updates.

SugarCheck is intended for adults and is not directed at children under 13. If you believe a child has sent personal information to eHUT, contact us so we can investigate and address it.

When practices change, eHUT will update this page and its effective date. The app and store listing should link to the current policy.
